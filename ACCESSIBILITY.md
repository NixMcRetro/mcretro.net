# Accessibility Statement

## My Commitment

McRetro.net is a personal website and historical archive covering retro computing, hardware repairs, digital preservation, software archaeology and the history of the internet.

I believe that access to information, technology and digital history should be available to as many people as possible, including people with disabilities.

My aim is to make McRetro.net accessible, readable and usable across different devices, browsers and assistive technologies, while preserving the historical character of the material.

## Accessibility Standards

I aim to work towards the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 Level AA for the modern website and its core functionality.

This is an ongoing goal, not a claim of formal compliance. McRetro.net has not undergone a comprehensive independent accessibility audit, and some areas may not yet meet these guidelines.

Accessibility improvements are considered as part of ongoing website maintenance and development.

## Current Accessibility Features

The modern website includes several features intended to improve usability:

- Responsive layouts for desktop computers, tablets and mobile devices.
- Structured HTML and headings to help organise information.
- Support for reduced-motion preferences in some website animations.
- Text-based articles and navigation that do not rely entirely on images.
- Accessible alternatives and descriptive text where they have been provided.

I continue to review navigation, readability, colour contrast, keyboard accessibility and compatibility with assistive technologies.

## Supported Environments

McRetro.net is designed for modern web browsers, including Firefox, Safari, Chrome and Edge, on desktop and mobile devices.

The website is also intended to remain usable with keyboard navigation, browser zoom and assistive technologies such as screen readers.

However, comprehensive compatibility testing across every browser, operating system and assistive technology has not been completed.

## Historical Content and Known Limitations

McRetro.net preserves material from different periods of computing and internet history, including restored websites, historical documents, software, images and videos.

Some older material was created before modern accessibility practices became widely adopted.

As a result:

- Historical websites may contain outdated HTML, unusual layouts or navigation that is difficult to use with assistive technologies.
- Some archived images may not have meaningful alternative text.
- Older videos may lack accurate captions or transcripts.
- Animated GIFs and other historical visual effects may not respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Certain interactive demonstrations and restored software may require specific browsers, input methods or display sizes.

Preserving the original material is an important part of the project, but I recognise that historical authenticity and accessibility do not always align.

Where practical, I aim to improve accessibility through additional descriptions, transcripts, alternative formats or explanatory notes without unnecessarily altering the original material.

## Reporting Accessibility Problems

If you experience an accessibility barrier on McRetro.net, I would appreciate hearing about it.

You can contact me through the channels listed on the [McRetro.net About page](https://mcretro.net/about/).

When reporting a problem, it is helpful to include:

- The page or feature affected.
- A description of the difficulty encountered.
- Your browser and operating system, if relevant.
- Any assistive technology being used, if you are comfortable sharing that information.
- Suggestions for improving accessibility or providing an alternative.

You do not need to disclose a disability or medical condition to report an accessibility problem.

## Ongoing Improvements

McRetro.net is independently maintained, and accessibility improvements are made alongside preservation, technical maintenance and new content.

I will consider reported barriers, prioritise problems that prevent people from accessing essential content or functionality, and investigate practical solutions.

Although I cannot guarantee that every issue will be resolved immediately, accessibility feedback is welcome and will help guide future improvements.

## Closing

Preserving digital history is only useful if people can explore and understand it.

My goal is to keep the personality and history of McRetro.net intact while making the experience more accessible to everyone.
