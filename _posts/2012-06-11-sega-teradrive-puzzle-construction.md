Next up we have the mysterious Puzzle Construction software. It definitely does interact with the Mega Drive side of the TeraDrive. Sega's own hardware archive identifies Puzzle Construction as dedicated TeraDrive software, and later preservation work has confirmed that its PC-side editor makes use of the machine's ability to communicate with the Mega Drive hardware. That explains why the sound and video effects seemed unusually advanced for an ordinary 286 PC. The exact division of work between the 286 and 68000 for every part of the software is more complicated, so I would not reduce it to simply saying one processor runs everything. The Sega licensing screen also makes rather more sense in hindsight because this was official Sega software specifically released for the TeraDrive.

{% include youtube.html id="X3XtOCb6LLE" %}

This last video is showing the Background Music Edit Panel. There are others for layout, rules, etc as well. This was the main one that I found to be enjoyable as there is lots of music to pick from including my personal favourite, Game 1.

All this work was done on my Model 3 TeraDrive although I did start tinkering with Puzzle Construction on my Model 2 with an IBM WDL-330P just sitting on top of the power supply. As soon as it was packed away I dug out the Model 3. It sure was a weekend to remember.
