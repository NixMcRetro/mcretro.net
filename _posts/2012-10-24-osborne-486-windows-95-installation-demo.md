Finally got the video together. Windows 95 is installed and running on the Osborne 486.

The CD-ROM drive works most of the time, but it is extremely selective about burned discs. With some burned discs, the system also tended to lock up. In my testing, the TDK Gold 80-minute CD-Rs worked more often than not. Those particular discs were manufactured by CMC Magnetics. My Taiyo Yuden discs would not read at all. That is just what this old drive did with the discs I had available. It isn't enough evidence to conclude that one media manufacturer was generally better than the other.

Unfortunately I had around fifty Taiyo Yudens and four... make that three TDK Gold discs left.

### Windows 95 comes to life

{% include youtube.html id="rysI5VD_lgE" %}

Here's another recording showing the installation and first startup. The old Osborne is chugging away, the hard drive is making plenty of noise, and the Creative CD-200 is actually reading discs. Miracles do happen!

Of course, this is Windows 95 on a 486, so the first boot isn't exactly a quick affair. There's the memory test, the setup screens, an awful lot of waiting and approximately 170 MB already used on the roughly 300 MB hard drive. I installed all the screensavers too. Priorities!

Eventually the familiar teal desktop appears and, would you believe it, the sound works. I even get to try playing an audio CD. The CD-200's activity lights distinguish between audio and data access, which is a neat little detail.

The recording also catches the period when I was worried about a boot-sector virus and having trouble with a network card. That was another problem to sort out, and I've documented the virus cleanup separately.

Still, Windows 95 is alive, the CD-ROM drive can read discs, and the machine is making progress. Slow progress, admittedly, but that's rather appropriate for a 486.
