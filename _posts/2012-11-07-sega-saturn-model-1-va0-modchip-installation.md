Another modification for the Sega Saturn, and this time we're working with one of the early PAL Model 1 machines. This particular Saturn has the VA0-style CD-board arrangement, which makes it rather different from some of the later revisions I've been playing around with.

I'd purchased a modchip from Rob at Console Goods in the United Kingdom. It was one of the universal types with connectors for both 20-pin and 21-pin ribbon cables, which was rather convenient given the different Saturn hardware revisions floating around.

Inside the machine, the early VA0 layout is immediately obvious. The power supply is mounted up in the lid rather than sitting in the same position as on the later models, and the CD mechanism has its own arrangement of cables and connectors.

The modchip itself was fairly straightforward to prepare. The board had a 5 V power connection and the appropriate ribbon-cable sockets, so most of the work involved getting everything connected neatly enough to fit back inside the console.

I also wanted to make sure the back of the modchip wouldn't short against the metal shielding. I ended up using some Kapton tape and a piece of cardboard to insulate it. Perhaps not the most glamorous arrangement, but those little component legs were rather pointy, and I didn't particularly want them poking through the tape.

The ribbon connectors weren't positioned quite as conveniently as I'd hoped, either. If they'd been facing the other way, the modchip could have sat much more neatly inside the machine. Instead, I had to work around the available space and make sure the cables weren't being crushed when I closed the lid.

The 5 V connection came from the Saturn's power supply connector. That also meant working around a mains-powered assembly, so I kept clear of the exposed power-supply components and concentrated on the identified low-voltage connection.

As with any Saturn modchip installation, the board revision matters. This early 20-pin arrangement isn't the same as the later 21-pin CD boards, and a wiring layout intended for one version shouldn't automatically be copied onto another.

Fortunately, the completed installation worked! I tested it with *Radiant Silvergun*, which I'd patched for use on my PAL Saturn, followed by *Guardian Heroes*. Both provided a rather nice demonstration of the machine running with the modchip installed.

I did wonder whether I might be better at *Radiant Silvergun* if I wasn't trying to play it with one hand while holding a camera. Probably not, but it's a convenient excuse. So that's another Saturn modification completed, and one more early VA0 machine ready for some gaming.
