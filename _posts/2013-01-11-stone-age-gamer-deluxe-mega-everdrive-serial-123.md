A quick look at my [Stone Age Gamer](https://stoneagegamer.com/) Deluxe Mega EverDrive, serial number 123. This post is mostly about the packaging and presentation rather than another full hardware demonstration, but the cartridge itself is one of the early Mega EverDrive units from the period.

I've just received it from the United States, and they've done a lovely job with the whole package. The cartridge itself has a beautiful black shell, with the Deluxe branding and serial number displayed on the front. Serial 123. Not bad at all!

There's quite a bit included in the box. We have the instruction manual, a Stone Age Gamer sticker, a Mega EverDrive sticker, a fridge magnet, and an SD card, which I opted to include with the order. There's also a USB cable and the cartridge itself, held neatly in place.

Even the little paper wrapper around the cartridge is rather entertaining. They've given it a suitably dramatic introduction, describing a cartridge rebuilt into something much more powerful. I think I'll keep that! And Velcro. Very 1980s, or at least it ought to be.

The contemporary Mega EverDrive manual from March 2012 documents PCB revision v1.00 with features including SD-card storage, MegaKey region handling, an in-game menu, experimental snapshot saves, Sega CD BIOS loading, and support for Mega Drive, Master System, and 32X software modes.

I'd already been using the Mega EverDrive for some of my more questionable hardware experiments, including loading a Mega-CD BIOS while investigating the Mega PC and TeraDrive. This Deluxe package is simply a much nicer home for it.

Unfortunately, there's one small problem with demonstrating it today. I don't actually have a Mega Drive handy! So this is more of a look at what Stone Age Gamer included than a test of the cartridge itself.

I even rather like the shipping box, with its little Sega-themed touches and references to blast processing. They've clearly put some effort into making this feel like something special. Thanks to Stone Age Gamer for putting together such a nice package, and of course to Krikzz for making the Mega EverDrive in the first place.

Now I just need a Mega Drive to put it in.
