**All your base are belong to us.** That's right, it's Zero Wing on the Sega Mega Drive, running through the XRGB-mini Framemeister. I wanted to show off the scanlines, but the footage also provides a fairly convincing demonstration of why I spend more time fixing hardware than playing shoot-'em-ups.

I'm not especially good at Zero Wing. I lose track of things, make questionable decisions about power-ups and occasionally seem surprised when flying straight into enemy fire ends badly. Still, the scanlines look fantastic and somehow I eventually made it further than I'd managed before. Clearly the Framemeister has improved my gaming ability. There can be no other explanation.

The second video is where things get particularly exciting, because I actually reached stage two. Before getting there, I discovered that collecting more of the green homing-missile power-ups made them increasingly useful. Naturally, I managed to lose them before defeating the first stage boss and had to finish the job with ordinary red shots instead.

I was rather pleased with that result. It was the furthest I'd reached in the game, and I even discovered another mini-boss that I hadn't encountered previously. Unfortunately, stage two soon reminded me that one successful run doesn't suddenly make me an expert.

At least the second video looks better. I managed to focus the camera properly this time, which is always a helpful feature when you're attempting to demonstrate video quality. My bad!

{% include youtube.html id="NVLMLNnh6WQ" %}

I've also decided to ditch the little intro sequences from my videos. My old workflow involved importing the camera footage into iMovie, editing and exporting it, then uploading the finished file to YouTube for another round of transcoding. Given that YouTube is going to re-encode everything anyway, I'd rather avoid an unnecessary extra generation of video processing.

Perhaps I should move to more capable editing software at some point, but I still haven't found anything quite as easy to use as iMovie for this sort of quick work. Unfortunately, iMovie also has more bugs than a large rainforest, so there's a price to pay for that convenience.

Enjoy the videos, admire those lovely scanlines and try not to judge my Zero Wing skills too harshly. I'm clearly much better at fixing the consoles than surviving the games.
