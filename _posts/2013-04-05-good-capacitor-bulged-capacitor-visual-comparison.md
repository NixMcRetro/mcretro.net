Here's a useful visual comparison between a brand-new electrolytic capacitor and one that has failed badly enough to bulge at the top. I've got two capacitors here, one in rather better condition than the other. It's quite interesting seeing them side by side because the damage is very obvious once you know what you're looking for.

Some aluminium electrolytic capacitors have a scored pressure vent on the top. If internal pressure builds up under abnormal electrical or thermal conditions, that weakened section is designed to open in a more controlled fashion.

Not every capacitor has that same sort of top vent, though. The 680 uF, 4 V parts I'm showing here have a different construction, and the failed one has started bulging around the bottom instead.

It's quite a sight. The rubber seal and plastic sleeve are being pushed out, and the capacitor is no longer sitting the way it should. Thankfully this particular one hasn't done anything especially spectacular, but it's certainly ready for replacement.

A swollen or opened pressure vent is an obvious warning sign. The internal pressure of an aluminium electrolytic capacitor can rise under abnormal electrical or thermal conditions, causing the safety vent to deform or open.

One important catch: **a capacitor does not have to bulge before it is bad.** Electrolytics can lose capacitance, develop excessive losses or leakage, or dry out without presenting such a wonderfully obvious visual clue.

The replacement capacitor is actually the same brand and rating as the failed one. Normally I'd have a look at the available alternatives, but in this case it was the only suitable part I had available. So the damaged capacitor in this video is an easy diagnosis. The innocent-looking ones can be considerably more annoying.
