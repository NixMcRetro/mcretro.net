Hop aboard for a very quick look inside an old Apple IIc. Sure does look antique! Better off in a museum than on a shelf.

My camera battery was about to go flat, so I thought I'd quickly record the machine while it was in pieces. I've never actually taken one of these apart before, and it is quite a lovely little computer inside.

Unfortunately, this particular IIc isn't behaving properly. It has a power problem that causes the machine to shut down, so I thought I'd inspect the power supply and see if anything looked obviously damaged or shorted.

Apple introduced the IIc in 1984 as a compact version of the Apple II idea. Rather than relying on the expansion slots that defined machines such as the IIe, Apple squeezed a surprising amount into the little case.

The IIc uses a 65C02 processor and shipped with 128 KB of RAM, while functionality for the disk controller, serial ports, mouse, and 80-column display was integrated into the machine rather than requiring the usual collection of expansion cards.

That explains why there is considerably less empty space and card-cage chaos inside than I am used to seeing in older computers.

The motherboard itself is rather beautiful. Everything is lined up neatly, with chips arranged in rows and plenty of labels printed on the PCB. Even the socketed components look tidy. Almost makes me feel bad about some of my other computers!

I also had a quick look at the capacitors while I was in there. This was during one of my capacitor-obsessed weekends, so naturally I was checking everything that looked remotely electrolytic.

Surprisingly, most of them looked quite reasonable. There's a particularly large red capacitor towards the back that caught my attention, although nothing jumped out as obviously failed during this initial inspection.

The power supply is a separate internal module, which should make it easier to remove and examine. There's also the built-in floppy drive, keyboard connector, and a collection of other connections around the board. Compact by 1984 standards. Still gloriously beige by ours.

This is only a quick internal look rather than a full restoration or teardown. I'll give it a clean, investigate the power fault, and see whether I can get it to turn on reliably again.
