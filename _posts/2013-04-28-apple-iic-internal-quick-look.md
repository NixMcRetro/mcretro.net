Apple introduced the IIc in 1984 as a compact version of the Apple II idea.

Rather than relying on the expansion slots that defined machines such as the IIe, Apple squeezed a surprising amount into the little case.

The IIc uses a 65C02 processor and shipped with 128 KB of RAM, while functionality for the disk controller, serial ports, mouse and 80-column display was integrated into the machine rather than requiring the usual collection of expansion cards.

That explains why there is considerably less empty space and card-cage chaos inside than I am used to seeing in older computers.

Compact by 1984 standards.

Still gloriously beige by ours.

This is only a quick internal look rather than a full restoration or teardown, but it is worth documenting what Apple managed to pack into the case.
