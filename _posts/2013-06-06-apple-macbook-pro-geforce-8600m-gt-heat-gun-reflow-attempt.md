Here we have a video of me bringing a 17-inch MacBook Pro back to life with a heat gun.

It did revive this particular machine.

That is the firsthand result.

It should not be confused with a proper repair procedure.

{% include youtube.html id="iP4HwYD9LaQ" %}

These MacBook Pro models were among the machines Apple identified as potentially affected by failures involving the NVIDIA GeForce 8600M GT.

Apple's remedy was hardware service or logic-board replacement, not reheating the GPU area with a handheld heat gun.

Back when I made this video, I wrote that the heat treatment might keep the machine running for somewhere between three and twelve months, and suggested that adding flux could make the result last longer.

I do not have evidence for a predictable lifespan like that.

Adding flux also does not turn uncontrolled heat-gun treatment into proper BGA rework.

A handheld heat gun provides broad, poorly controlled heating and can damage nearby components, warp the board or create additional faults.

So the correct way to describe this experiment is:

**the heat treatment temporarily revived this particular failed MacBook Pro.**

Nothing more universal than that.

I later tried a similar heat-gun experiment on a PC video card and, at this point in 2013, was considering trying the same thing on a PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360.

That remains part of the historical experiment.

It is not a recommendation to repair those machines this way.

Still, temporarily seeing this MacBook Pro come back to life was rather satisfying.
