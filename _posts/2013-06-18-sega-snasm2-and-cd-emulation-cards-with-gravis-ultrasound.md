This video was more or less put together for my own reference while trying to get the Sega Mega-CD development cards working on a Pentium III 800 MHz system.

Electrically, the setup appeared compatible as far as I could tell.

Physically, I simply did not have a PC case large enough for all that development-card absurdity.

![](/assets/images/2013/img_0366.jpg)

So we worked our way down the food chain to a Pentium 100 MHz.

What's that?

Now it doesn't work properly at all?

Bah!

Moving down the chain even further brought us to the humble 486DX4-100.

It works a charm.

Throw in a Gravis UltraSound and you've got quite a workstation!

![](/assets/images/2013/img_0408.jpg)

Later testing showed that one Pentium-class motherboard could be made to cooperate when I disabled the CPU's internal cache in BIOS, although I never established why.

So host compatibility clearly involved more than simply asking whether the PC was fast enough.
