Good to see I got nowhere with this, another dead video card is all I ever seem to have! :D

I'd picked up another old video card for the princely sum of $1, with postage costing about eight times as much. Unfortunately, it was producing some rather interesting graphical rubbish, and I suspected the video RAM might be responsible.

I had a few other cards lying around, so naturally the next step was to start borrowing RAM chips and swapping them about. Different speeds, different cards, different results, but nothing that actually fixed the troublesome board. So much for a quick and easy repair!

There was also a little corrosion on the back of the card, and some fairly questionable solder joints. I gave things a clean and tried a few more experiments before deciding I'd had quite enough of that particular problem for one evening.

Instead, I moved some of the RAM onto another Trident-based card, which was rather more cooperative and reported 2048 KB of video memory. That would do nicely for the time being.

Meanwhile, the 486 workstation was becoming quite an elaborate contraption. With the IntelDX4 OverDrive running at 100 MHz, I was trying to fit the Sega Mega-CD development hardware, Gravis UltraSound, Sound Blaster Pro 2 and assorted controller cards into the available slots.

A shorter GoldStar Prime 2 IDE controller proved especially useful, allowing my 2 GB disk-on-module to work without occupying quite so much space. The VLB cards and that enormous OverDrive heatsink certainly weren't making the job any easier!

By the end of the video, the machine was taking shape and most of the hardware was cooperating. There was still work to do with the optical drives and some configuration issues, but at least we were getting somewhere with the computer itself. The original troublesome video card, however, wasn't quite so fortunate.
