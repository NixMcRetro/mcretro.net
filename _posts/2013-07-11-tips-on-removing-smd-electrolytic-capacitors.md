Here's a quick look at the method I was using to remove surface-mount electrolytic capacitors from old circuit boards. I've been doing quite a bit of SNES repair work lately, and these little capacitors have been keeping me busy. Some come away without too much trouble. Others have rather different ideas!

In the video I'm demonstrating the rocking or wobbling technique I'd been using at the time. The idea was to work the capacitor loose, leaving its leads behind on the solder pads. It had worked for me on quite a few boards, but I'd already managed to lift two pads, which is a fairly good reminder that there's a risk involved.

The first was on the very first capacitor I removed. Whoops! The second was on a Super Nintendo where corrosion had weakened the pad and trace around a leaking capacitor. Fortunately, there was another point on the same connection nearby, so I could make a repair rather than give up on the board.

I also found that using pliers could make it easier to apply more force than intended, increasing the chance of damaging the pads or traces. It worked for me often enough at the time, but those damaged pads made me think twice about the technique. It's easy to apply too much force, particularly with pliers, so I'd be cautious about treating it as a reliable approach. Supporting the board and properly freeing the soldered leads is the safer approach.

You can't win them all, but it's nice when there's a nearby via or solder point that lets you save a damaged connection. Feel free to share any tips or pointers in the comments. I'm always happy to have some feedback!
