Zip, zap and away! The idea here is simple: replace the soldered battery in a Super Nintendo cartridge with a holder, so the next battery replacement doesn't involve desoldering anything. This particular cartridge is NHL '96, a PAL game that will eventually become an EarthBound cartridge if everything goes according to plan.

The cartridge has battery-backed SRAM for saving games, and I'm fitting a holder for a CR2032 cell. I've got a couple of different styles to choose from, including one where the battery slides in from the side. Unfortunately, the first holder doesn't sit quite as nicely as I'd hoped. It's a little too tall for the cartridge shell.

That's rather annoying, because the electrical side of the idea is straightforward enough. A battery holder makes replacement much easier, but only if you can still close the cartridge afterwards! I may need to modify the back of this particular shell, although it would be nicer to find a holder with enough clearance in the first place.

There's also a MAD-1 chip and 64K of SRAM on the board, along with another capacitor that I'll probably replace while I'm here. You know how I am with capacitors! I've already been looking at the cartridge contacts too, since the amount of wear suggests somebody actually played NHL '96 at some point. Amazing!

This is for the upcoming EarthBound cartridge I'm making from terrible EA Sports games. If you object, you have no soul! Let's face it, your old saves probably weren't that good anyway. Do better this time, you've got ten years... Go!
