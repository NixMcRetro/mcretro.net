Is there anything I won't put on video? Probably a few things, although apparently my multimeter probes falling to pieces isn't one of them! We've also got some more Aiwa Mega-CD investigation, a few complaints about my equipment and, naturally, a random cat at the end.

The red multimeter probe was almost ready to fall off, and the black one actually separated when I left the leads hanging over the side of the workbench. Excellent strain relief on those things! I suppose that's what you get for buying cheap probes, although I didn't expect them to retire quite so spectacularly.

Fortunately, I'd already ordered a replacement multimeter. It's a VC97, or something along those lines, which cost about $40 Australian delivered. One feature I'm rather looking forward to is automatic ranging, so I won't have to spend quite so much time switching between voltage and resistance ranges before realising I've selected the wrong one. At least that's the theory!

Unfortunately, without a working multimeter, the Aiwa Mega-CD repair is on hold. I'd been investigating a simplified power-supply arrangement that wouldn't have to pass through the battery board. That seemed worth pursuing, particularly given the rather strange condition of one of the boards.

One battery board from the revision 2 unit was still in one piece, while the other had somehow cracked in several places. Considering it's mounted safely inside the Aiwa, I haven't the faintest idea how it ended up like that! I also noticed what appeared to be different suffix markings, -8 and -10, although I hadn't established exactly what those differences represented.

I've also been looking at other Aiwa boomboxes that might be useful for parts. I found a Japanese unit that looked remarkably similar to the Mega-CD-equipped CSD-GM1, apart from its charcoal-coloured case and the missing Mega-CD and karaoke hardware. The example appeared to be around two years newer, roughly 1996 rather than 1994, so I was interested in what ordinary boombox parts might be shared between them.

At the time I was trying to work out the model number from the photographs, and I was even considering importing one through Yahoo Japan Auctions. Thanks to NJRoadfan for subsequently identifying the Aiwa CSD-GM1's cousin as the CSD-EX110 or CSD-EX111 in the United States. That certainly gives me a more useful starting point for the search!

Shipping an entire boombox from Japan probably isn't going to be cheap, especially considering how large these things are. Still, having another example to compare against might be rather handy, provided I can find one for a sensible price.

I've also got a proper ESR meter on the way from the United States, which should be useful when I can finally get back to the repairs. It was already making its way across the Pacific when I recorded this, so at least the equipment situation was starting to improve.

And you can tell I'm getting the hang of iMovie 11 now that pictures are appearing in the video! There are even some circuit-bending photographs in there, perhaps a little hint of something else I'll be playing around with soon.

As for the random cat, well, why not? Wouldn't be a proper McRetro production without something completely unrelated wandering into the story, would it?
