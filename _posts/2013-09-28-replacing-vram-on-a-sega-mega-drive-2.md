Replacing the VRAM in Mega Drive 2 serial **440005313** didn't solve the issue, but boy did I have fun doing it! And that's what this YouTube channel is all about - Fun!

This particular Mega Drive 2 had been suffering from intermittent video problems and illegal instruction errors. I'd originally considered turning it into a circuit-bending experiment, but then discovered I had a spare RAM chip from another water-damaged Mega Drive. Might as well try replacing it first!

Getting the old chip off was an adventure in itself. I tried using a piece of wire underneath the pins while heating them with the soldering iron, but that wasn't going particularly well. In fact, I was mostly bending the legs. Out came the hot-air gun instead.

With the replacement chip positioned and tacked down, it was time for plenty of flux, solder and cleaning up the inevitable bridges between pins. The first test produced a wonderful assortment of blue, white and black screens. At least it was doing something!

A bit more soldering eventually got Sonic running again, which looked rather promising. Unfortunately, the original intermittent problem wasn't permanently resolved by the VRAM replacement. Still, it was an entertaining exercise in removing and replacing one of these chips, and there were more things to investigate.
