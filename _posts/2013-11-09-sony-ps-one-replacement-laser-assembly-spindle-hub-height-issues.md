Just a word of warning to anyone else buying 'new' laser assemblies from China or Hong Kong eBay sellers. Of about eight replacement assemblies I bought, only two seemed to hold the hub on properly... but only just!

I'm working on a Sony PS one SCPH-102 with some rather unpleasant liquid damage. To make things even more interesting, the original optical drive is missing the little spindle hub that holds the disc in place. How that happened is anybody's guess!

I ordered a complete replacement drive assembly for around $8 delivered. It seemed like a reasonably good deal, particularly since replacing the hub alone wasn't going to save me much money. Unfortunately, the replacement didn't look particularly new, with some rather suspicious marks around its connections.

After installing the assembly, the laser attempted to focus and the disc started spinning, but the console still wouldn't recognise anything. Since this machine had already suffered liquid damage, I wasn't prepared to blame the replacement laser just yet.

Fortunately, I had another PS one available for testing. This machine didn't have the same liquid-damage problems, so I installed the replacement drive and gave it another go. Would you believe it, Need for Speed actually loaded! At least we knew the laser could read something.

I then tried a known-working assembly in the liquid-damaged console, but it still refused to recognise discs. That suggested there were additional problems with the console itself, rather than simply a faulty optical pickup.

Unfortunately, the replacement drive wasn't finished causing trouble. During further testing, the disc appeared to scrape against the mechanism, and the spindle hub wasn't staying where it belonged. Eventually the thing came loose entirely. Wonderful!

At the time, I suspected the motor spindle might have been too short, but I didn't take measurements to establish that. The hub could have been seated incorrectly, or there may have been some other dimensional difference in these replacement assemblies.

So while the laser was capable of reading a game, the mechanical quality left rather a lot to be desired. As for the liquid-damaged PlayStation, that'll need another investigation before it's going to be playing anything again.
