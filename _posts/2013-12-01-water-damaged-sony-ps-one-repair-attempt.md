Water-damaged consoles are the worst of the bunch. Unpredictable and filthy... and then there's the rust!

This little PS one would power on, but it wouldn't recognise any discs. There was corrosion around the controller ports, near the power jack and even underneath the metal shielding. After cleaning up the board, I discovered that the power switch was in a rather sorry state, with plenty of rust inside. Out it came, and in went a replacement switch I had lying around. Not exactly factory original, but it did the job!

With the controller and memory card responding properly, things were looking a little better. Unfortunately, the optical drive wasn't doing much of anything. After checking the power connections and cleaning corrosion from the ribbon cable connector, I finally managed to get the laser mechanism moving and attempting to focus. We even had the disc spinning! Progress, although the console still refused to recognise a game, even with a known-good drive assembly.

Audio CDs weren't working either, so there was obviously more trouble lurking somewhere. I considered trying a modchip as a last-ditch experiment, but wasn't particularly convinced it would help. Meanwhile, the rusty metal shielding got a vinegar bath followed by a good scrub with aluminium foil and a toothbrush, which actually worked rather nicely. The metal was looking considerably healthier than the PlayStation at this point!
