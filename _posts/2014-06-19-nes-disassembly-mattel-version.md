Just some poking around inside a Nintendo Entertainment System! The good old PAL-A Mattel variety sold in Australia.

I thought I'd pull this one apart and see what was hiding underneath all that grey plastic. I've spent plenty of time with Sega hardware, but the NES is a slightly different beast. How complicated could it possibly be?

First up, there are six screws holding the outer case together. The plastic is getting rather brittle with age, so a little care is needed. There's also an expansion port hiding underneath the console, which is an interesting little curiosity.

Once inside, we're greeted by an impressive collection of metal shielding and that rather unusual cartridge-loading mechanism. Nintendo certainly went to a lot of trouble keeping everything enclosed. There are plenty more screws to remove before we can finally get a good look at the motherboard.

Underneath all that metal is the PAL Mattel board, complete with its CPU, RAM and assorted other chips. There's also a rather large capacitor near the power circuitry and a collection of connectors running to the controller ports, power switch and reset button.

One of the screws looked as though it hadn't been put back properly, so I suspect this particular NES may have been opened before. We'll find out a little more about that in a later video.

Not a bad little exploration! Next up we'll have a closer look at the cartridge connector, because those things can be particularly troublesome after a few decades of use.
