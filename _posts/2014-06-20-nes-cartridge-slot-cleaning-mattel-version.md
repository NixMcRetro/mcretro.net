We delve a little deeper into the NES and have a look at that rather unusual cartridge connector. It's certainly an interesting arrangement compared with the cartridge slots found in most other consoles!

After removing the loading mechanism, I could finally get at the contacts properly. Dirty connections are one possible reason for the familiar flashing red light or blank screen when trying to play a game. Let's see what we can do about that.

I started with an eraser to clean the exposed contacts on the motherboard. They didn't look particularly filthy at first, but the amount of grime coming off was quite impressive! A little isopropyl alcohol helped clean away the remaining residue.

For the cartridge connector itself, I used a card wrapped in cleaning material with some isopropyl alcohol. Sliding it in and out of the connector gradually brought more dirt out of the contacts. Remember to clean your game cartridges too, otherwise you're only going to put some of that grime straight back in!

While everything was apart, I also had a look at the electrolytic capacitors on this CPU-11 board. The three smaller ones were marked 100 µF at 6.3 V, 2.2 µF at 50 V and 1 µF at 50 V. There was also a much larger capacitor in the power and RF section.

Replacement cartridge connectors were available for around $10 delivered on eBay at the time, although the example I handled didn't feel quite as sturdy as Nintendo's original. Its outer metal casing came away rather more easily than I'd have liked.

With the connector cleaned, it's time to move on to the capacitors. We'll see how the machine behaves once everything is back together!
