We'll be taking a proper look into Sega Dreamcast development hardware. I did not actually own a retail Dreamcast at this point. I did, however, own an **HKT-0120 Dreamcast / Katana development box**. Curious.

It's quite a sizeable beast, and there's even a Sega of America asset tag on the top. Not something you see every day! I thought this was going to make for an interesting couple of weeks.

The HKT-0120 belongs to Sega's later Set 5 development hardware and was designed to work with a host PC over SCSI as part of the Dreamcast development environment.

Before trying to make the unit useful again, I wanted a raw copy of the internal hard drive in case there were any goodies hiding on it. You know, potentially interesting development material. ;) The next videos will therefore focus on imaging that drive before I start making changes to the machine.

### Update 21st November 2026: Where This Development Box Came From

I've dug up some more of the original purchase details for this HKT-0120. The **final amount I paid, including shipping to Australia, was A$1,544.18**. The transaction details also record **US$1,118.25** at an exchange rate of **1 AUD = US$0.724171**. I haven't found the actual payment date, so I'm not going to pretend the exchange rate tells us exactly when I bought it. I certainly had the machine by the time this video appeared in March 2016.

According to the seller, they'd bought the development box through Craigslist at least five years earlier, from someone who worked in the games industry. The sales description associated this particular kit with **Visual Concepts**, the studio behind games including *Ooga Booga* and *Sonic Adventure 2*. However, the seller wasn't sure whether the person they'd bought it from had actually used the unit or worked for Visual Concepts. It's an interesting bit of possible history, but not proven ownership.

The seller also told a rather painful story about a **GameCube NPDP reader** that was supposed to have been part of that earlier hardware lot. Apparently the previous owner threw away both the reader and its cartridge before the sale, worried that the cartridge contained sensitive data. This was already well into the Wii era! Couldn't the cartridge have been wiped instead of throwing out the whole lot? Ouch. :)

The listing described the Dreamcast kit as working and included an installed hard drive, power cable, VGA cable, and controller. It also mentioned its VGA output, region selection, and ability to read GD-R discs. The seller said they hadn't changed anything on the hard drive, although they didn't know what might be on it. Naturally, that last detail was exactly why I wanted to preserve the drive before experimenting with the machine.
