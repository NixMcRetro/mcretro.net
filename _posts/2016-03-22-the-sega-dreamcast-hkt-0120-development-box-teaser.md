We'll be taking a proper look into Sega Dreamcast development hardware. I did not actually own a retail Dreamcast at this point. I did, however, own an **HKT-0120 Dreamcast / Katana development box**. Curious.

It's quite a sizeable beast, and there's even a Sega of America asset tag on the top. Not something you see every day! I thought this was going to make for an interesting couple of weeks.

Getting it shipped to Australia wasn't exactly cheap. The **final amount I paid, including shipping, was A$1,544.18**. The transaction details record **US$1,118.25** at an exchange rate of **1 AUD = US$0.724171**. I haven't found the exact payment date, but I had the development box by the time I made this video in March 2016.

The seller had an interesting story about where it came from. They'd bought it through Craigslist at least five years earlier, from someone who worked in the games industry. The listing associated this particular devkit with **Visual Concepts**, the studio behind games such as *Ooga Booga* and *Sonic Adventure 2*. However, the seller couldn't confirm whether that earlier owner had actually worked for Visual Concepts or used the kit themselves. It's a tantalising bit of history, but I wouldn't call the studio connection proven.

And then there's the GameCube story. The seller said the earlier owner had been going to include a **GameCube NPDP reader** in that original lot of development hardware, but threw away both the reader and its cartridge before the sale because they were worried about sensitive data. This was well into the Wii era! Couldn't they have just wiped the cartridge rather than throwing the whole thing away? What a waste. :)

According to the listing, the HKT-0120 was working and came with a power cable, VGA cable, and controller. The hard drive was still installed, with the seller saying they hadn't changed its contents and didn't know what might be on it. The listing also described VGA output at 640 × 480, a rotary region selector, and support for GD-R discs. Naturally, I was particularly interested in that untouched hard drive!

The HKT-0120 belongs to Sega's later Set 5 development hardware and was designed to work with a host PC over SCSI as part of the Dreamcast development environment.

Before trying to make the unit useful again, I wanted a raw copy of the internal hard drive in case there were any goodies hiding on it. You know, potentially interesting development material. ;) The next videos will therefore focus on imaging that drive before I start making changes to the machine.
