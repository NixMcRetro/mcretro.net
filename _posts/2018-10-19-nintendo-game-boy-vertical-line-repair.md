Finally, a repair where nothing caught fire! I've got a couple of original Game Boys on the workbench, and one of them has a rather unpleasant collection of missing vertical lines on the LCD. It's difficult to enjoy Tetris when a sizeable chunk of the screen has decided it would rather not display anything.

The first job was getting the case apart, which requires Nintendo's wonderful tri-wing screws. I've got a couple of different screwdrivers that are supposed to fit these things, although naturally one is slightly more useful than the other. Once the back was removed, there were still more screws and a ribbon cable between me and the display.

Inside, the original Game Boy is a lovely little piece of hardware. There are several circuit boards, the LCD assembly, speaker and all the usual components you'd expect from Nintendo's early handheld design. I also took the opportunity to compare some of the differences between the units I had on hand.

The missing lines appeared to be related to the LCD ribbon connection rather than a completely dead screen. I'd read about attempts to restore contact by carefully warming the bonded connection, and decided to see whether I could persuade this one to behave. Of course, applying heat near a delicate display is something that can go horribly wrong if you aren't careful.

And naturally, I started with the soldering iron far too hot! At around 350 degrees Celsius, I managed to soften part of the material much more than I'd intended, and some of the lines actually became worse. Wonderful. That was a fairly convincing demonstration of what not to do.

After finally working out how to change the temperature setting on the Hakko, I reduced the heat substantially and tried again. With much gentler heat and careful positioning, the missing columns gradually began to reappear. It was rather satisfying watching the picture come back together after the earlier attempt had made such a mess.

Once the display looked right, I gave it another test. Excellent news! The missing lines were gone and Tetris was once again looking like Tetris. I was surprised at how well it worked, particularly considering I'd been rather less gentle with the ribbon at the beginning of the experiment.

Reassembly turned into another little adventure when I realised I needed some thin double-sided tape. The sort with a thick foam layer wasn't what I wanted for keeping the LCD's surrounding parts neatly in place. A trip to the shops later, I finally had something suitable and could get the machine back together.

There was a final round of testing once everything was reassembled, and the screen still looked beautiful. The picture wasn't perfectly centred in the window, but that was my own assembly work rather than the original missing-column fault. At least the important part had worked!

This was a successful repair on this particular Game Boy, but it's not a universal cure for every LCD with missing lines. Excessive heat can damage the ribbon bond or display permanently, as I nearly demonstrated myself. Still, it's nice to have a proper success on the bench for a change, with no flames required.

...and I might have uploaded an unboxing video of a camera that I have since sold on after coming to the realisation that my iPhone is just as capable when on a Joby phone holder coupled with a cheap stand.
