This one sure was a interesting... but I lost most of the footage and just ended up with what you see in the above video. At least the darned thing works, which is really the important part when you've got a CD player that refuses to eject its tray!

The machine in question is a Denon DCD-F101, and the original problem was rather straightforward. The tray wouldn't come out properly because the little drive belt in the mechanism had deteriorated. The rubber had reached the point where it wasn't doing the job anymore, so something had to be found to take its place.

My first solution was a rubber band I happened to find in a drawer. Extremely professional, I know! But it was good enough to get the eject mechanism moving again, which proved that the belt was the problem. Sometimes you just need something simple to work out whether the rest of the mechanism is still capable of doing its thing.

Of course, the first rubber band was the wrong colour for such a magnificent piece of machinery. I couldn't possibly leave it like that, could I? So the second repair involved fitting a black rubber band instead. Much more appropriate for a high-end CD player, and the tray was happily opening and closing again.

A proper replacement drive belt with the correct dimensions would be the better long-term solution. A household rubber band isn't necessarily designed for the same tension, durability or operating conditions. But this little experiment did identify the problem and demonstrate that the mechanism could still operate.
