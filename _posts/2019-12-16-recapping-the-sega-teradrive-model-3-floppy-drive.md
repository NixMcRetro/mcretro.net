The hard drives are all recapped, so we move along to the floppy drives. Ronnie from ASSEMbler Games had warned me about their leaky capacitors, and given how much work I've already put into these TeraDrives, I'd rather get them replaced before they cause any more trouble.

The Model 3 has just one floppy drive, so this should be a reasonably simple introduction before we tackle the two drives in the Model 2. Simple, of course, is a relative term when you're dealing with thirty-year-old electronics.

After removing what seemed like an endless collection of screws and connectors, I finally reached the circuit board. There are two surface-mount electrolytic capacitors to replace: a 47 µF, 16 V capacitor at C22 and a 10 µF, 16 V capacitor at C26.

I thought this would be a good opportunity to try out my desoldering tweezers. They certainly got the capacitors off, although one of the old components made rather a mess in the process. Fortunately, the solder pads survived, which was the important thing.

There was also some unpleasant green residue around parts of the board, suggesting that electrolyte had escaped from the old capacitors. After cleaning things up, I soldered the replacements into position and checked the polarity.

Reassembling the drive was another adventure entirely. Those little flexible cables and connectors really weren't interested in going back where they belonged. I spent considerably longer fighting with them than I'd expected, but eventually everything was back together.

That's one floppy drive recapped, with two more to go. We haven't established whether it works properly after reassembly just yet, but at least the capacitor replacement is complete!
