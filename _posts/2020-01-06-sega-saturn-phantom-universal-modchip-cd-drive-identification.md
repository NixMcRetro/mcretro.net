This week we've got a bunch of Phantom Universal videos recorded way back in 2016. Hopefully they're still relevant to the Saturns of today! With Rhea already around and the Satiator on the way, we're definitely getting closer to a CD-less society.

The Phantom Universal sits between the Saturn motherboard and CD-ROM drive, allowing compatible CD-R backups to boot. It doesn't remove the console's separate region lock, so games still need to match the region unless another solution is used. Before installing the chip, though, we need to work out which type of CD drive is inside the Saturn.

First, disconnect the console from power and make sure there's no disc left in the drive. There are five screws underneath the case, which can be removed with a suitable Phillips screwdriver. Once they're out, the top shell lifts away, giving us access to the CD mechanism.

With the cover removed, we can identify the drive without taking the entire console apart. In this particular Saturn I found a JVC mechanism, which I checked using the markings on the drive assembly. The circuit board layout and chip arrangement can also provide clues, although the part markings are a more useful starting point.

The important distinction is the ribbon cable. The common JVC arrangement uses a 21-pin connection, while the Sanyo version uses 20 pins. They aren't electrically interchangeable, even if the connectors look rather similar, so it's important to identify the drive before choosing the correct modchip installation instructions.

If you need to remove the drive assembly, the power and ribbon cables should be disconnected carefully. The drive sits on rubber mounts to reduce vibration, and the ribbon cable has exposed contacts on one side. I made a point of checking the orientation before removing it so everything could go back the same way.

One other thing to keep clear of is the power supply. Its capacitors can retain a dangerous charge even after the machine has been unplugged, and there's no need to be poking around that section just to identify the CD drive.

That's the drive identification sorted. The actual Phantom Universal installation comes next, once we've established which connector and instructions apply to this Saturn.
