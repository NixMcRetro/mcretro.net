Never saw myself as much of a mechanic, but here I am replacing a vacuum actuator in a motor vehicle which helps with fan control. This is chapter two of a five-part series on the Holden VY Series II. I tried a few things to get it working as best as possible. Some things I succeeded at, others I didn’t. Join me on this weird journey of retro car repair!

We found that the airflow kept diverting to the windscreen when other modes were selected, particularly under engine load above roughly 2000 RPM. The VY HVAC system uses inlet-manifold vacuum to operate its mode actuators, with a vacuum tank and check valve helping maintain vacuum when manifold vacuum drops. Replacing one actuator was an attempt to eliminate one possible leak, not an attempt to create more suction. We picked up a "Commodore Vacuum Actuator Air Vent Control VT VX VY VZ Genuine Holden NEW" for [$77 shipped](https://web.archive.org/web/20231014234426/https://www.ebay.com.au/itm/252971741077).

Other possible leak points still included the hoses, check valve, vacuum tank, remaining actuators and control hardware.
