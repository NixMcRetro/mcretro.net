![Glass paperweight with a miniature clear UTS Tower inside a dome resting on a wooden desk, beside a wristwatch.](/assets/images/2024/img_1358.jpg)

*A little UTS tower in a glass dome. Probably much easier to carry home than the real thing, and a nice reminder of a rather eventful few months.*
{: .media-caption }

Well, university has been a big false start. I've gone with option 4 from [the last blog post](/the-people-at-university-are-amazing/). That's dropping all subjects and reapplying to UAC after I get all my other stuff in order. My other stuff is my life... getting my life in order, should be an easy task right?

![Indoor selfie of an adult with shoulder-length brown hair and a black T-shirt, with a colourful abstract image on a monitor and hanging vines behind.](/assets/images/2024/img_1368.jpg)

*Another selfie at home in front of the colourful TV setup. I'd been trying to make sense of everything that happened over the past month.*
{: .media-caption }

I've been trialling medications (as always) and found I take fewer selfies of myself when not on pregabalin. I suppose I find that I am less accepting of myself. It's strange. Almost like my baseline self doesn't like me so much I need to be **_somewhat not me_**.

![Round foil-covered container on a grey countertop with handwritten words 'Sausages 2.0 Revised Edition'.](/assets/images/2024/img_1352.jpg)

*Sausages 2.0, Revised Edition. Yes, I apparently version-control the contents of the fridge now. Naturally!*
{: .media-caption }

That could be the autism talking though. That's right, I'm autistic. Which is actually kind of neat and explains a lot. I've had a few independent people suggest it and a psychiatrist confirm it. I am very happy with that. It helps me understand myself a little bit more.

![Tree-lined stone path with globe-topped lampposts leading towards a pale stone building, with sunlight filtering through the branches.](/assets/images/2024/img_1353.jpg)

*The trees outside Museum station were looking glorious that day. A bit of sunshine and greenery before dealing with everything else on my mind.*
{: .media-caption }

In the past month, I have had a very productive psychiatrist session. Let me just say these trees outside of Museum train station were more vibrant than they have ever been. In the session itself, trauma was unpacked. Organised and packed back away. It feels so strange to open up completely to a person external to your own self.

![Outdoor selfie of an adult with shoulder-length light-brown hair, black sunglasses and a black shirt beside a pale wall in bright sunlight.](/assets/images/2024/img_1354.jpg)

*A bit of September sunshine between appointments. At least the weather was being cooperative, even if the rest of life wasn't.*
{: .media-caption }

One year ago I abandoned my pursuit of the disability support pension (DSP) because my psychologist at the time didn't think it was feasible. Now I am seeing a psychiatrist who is more open to discussion on the subject rather than shooting me down. So we'll see where that leads.

![Black-and-white street art of an office worker on the phone, with the question 'Is the wealth and status my job provides worth this existential dread?' on a weathered utility box.](/assets/images/2024/img_1359.jpg)

*Even the electrical boxes are having an existential crisis. September really did have a theme.*
{: .media-caption }

Also had an appointment with my endocrinologist - all my blood work was looking good. I'd been taking 5,000 IU of vitamin D3 per day and my phosphate had risen. Related? Maybe, but that result alone couldn't tell me. The routine Australian adult upper intake level is 3,200 IU/day, though higher doses can be used with clinical monitoring. I was also discussing a 600,000 IU intramuscular vitamin D3 dose. That's a huge dose, and too much vitamin D can cause hypercalcaemia, so it was something to work through with the endocrinologist and blood tests.

![Back of a black Xbox 360 controller with its battery compartment open, showing two rechargeable AA batteries and a screwdriver inserted between them.](/assets/images/2024/img_1355.jpg)

*Time to inspect the gifted Xbox 360 controller. Leaky batteries really are the gift that keeps on giving.*
{: .media-caption }

I was given this controller as a gift to go with an Xbox I found on the side of the road in May. The batteries had leaked (as they do). So it needed an inspection and clean.

![Front of a black Xbox 360 controller with two thumbsticks, a directional pad, colourful A, B, X and Y buttons, and a green-ringed Xbox button.](/assets/images/2024/img_1356.jpg)

*From the front it looked perfectly normal. As usual, the interesting problems were hiding in the battery compartment.*
{: .media-caption }

The contacts were a bit corroded so I figured I'd fix it up.

![Back of a black Xbox 360 controller with the battery pack removed, exposing its contacts, with two loose metal springs on the work surface below.](/assets/images/2024/img_1357.jpg)

*The tiny battery-contact springs sitting beside the opened controller. Not exactly glamorous repair work, but at least it's fixable.*
{: .media-caption }

And fixed!

![Opened Xbox 360 console on a leaf-patterned desk mat beside a laptop showing a circuit board guide, with a small monitor and Pride flags behind.](/assets/images/2024/img_0828.jpg)

*The Xbox 360 on the workbench, with a soldering guide open on the laptop. Time to find out what sort of trouble I'd got myself into.*
{: .media-caption }

I sourced a new third party power supply and it actually worked! Armed with a working controller, power adapter and functional console... there was only one thing left to do. Bend it to my will. It must play all the games! Mod time!

![Magnified Xbox 360 circuit board on a small monitor, with thin black and yellow wires attached near labelled solder pads and components.](/assets/images/2024/img_1360.jpg)

*Those solder points were every bit as tiny as promised. The magnified view made things slightly less terrifying.*
{: .media-caption }

Oh good, really small points that need soldering. I mean I've softmodded a Wii before... and installed modchips into Xbox OG. How hard can an Xbox 360 be?

![Underside of an Xbox 360 motherboard with a metal X-shaped heat-sink bracket and a thin wire held in place by translucent yellow tape.](/assets/images/2024/img_1361.jpg)

*A little wiring on the underside of the Xbox 360 motherboard. All those tiny pads certainly kept things interesting.*
{: .media-caption }

Ehhhh, it was kind of annoying. PLL points and all that jazz.

![Opened Xbox 360 with its cooling fan exposed and a Raspberry Pi Pico board wired to the console motherboard with several purple leads.](/assets/images/2024/img_1362.jpg)

*The Raspberry Pi Pico finally got a job other than blinking LEDs. A whole lot of purple wiring later, the mod was taking shape.*
{: .media-caption }

![Television showing the bright blue 'XeLL RELOADED' Xbox 360 boot screen, with white console startup text below the logo.](/assets/images/2024/img_1363.jpg)

*And there it was - XeLL Reloaded on the television! The pile of tiny wires had apparently been worth the effort.*
{: .media-caption }

So much could have gone wrong...

![Television displaying the title screen of Burnout Revenge, including 'Press START to continue', with cables and an Xbox console nearby.](/assets/images/2024/img_1364.jpg)

*Burnout Revenge running on the Xbox 360. Seeing an actual game on screen after all that soldering was a very welcome sight!*
{: .media-caption }

But it didn't. It actually worked. I'm still capable of following guides and doing tech-related stuff. Then I remembered I had to drop uni. So I did. After census date, which creates a bit more hassle but nothing I can't probably handle.

![Dark assignment grading screen showing a score of 10 out of 10 points, with a class mean of 8.3 and a range from 0 to 10.](/assets/images/2024/img_1365.jpg)

*A full 10 out of 10 for a uni assignment, arriving on the very day I submitted my request to withdraw. Of course it did!*
{: .media-caption }

And then of course this result comes through on the day I submit my request to drop my degree. I bet I get a lower mark next time around. Ha ha!

![Television playing The Owl House cartoon on a black shelving unit surrounded by trailing vines and warm fairy lights.](/assets/images/2024/img_1366.jpg)

*I ended up binge-watching The Owl House, surrounded by plants and fairy lights. What an excellent show!*
{: .media-caption }

Amazing show, I highly recommend it for anyone. Season 3 was just the icing on the cake.

![Small pale golden cicada-shaped shell clinging to rough tree bark beside a suburban street.](/assets/images/2024/img_1367.jpg)

*A tiny cicada sign on a tree - spring was getting started, even if the bigger noisy ones were still to come.*
{: .media-caption }

The first cicadas of spring have appeared, the itty bitty Silver Princess cicadas. The larger cicadas aren't due until mid-November if my calendar is correct.

![Tiny brown skink peeking between cupped fingers with black-painted fingernails, showing its head and one small foreleg.](/assets/images/2024/img_1370.jpg)

*Another tiny skink making an appearance. The little ones still manage to find all the most inconvenient hiding spots!*
{: .media-caption }

Until they appear, we have plenty of skinks in my window frames nearly being squished every time I open the window...

![Small grey-brown lizard peeking upwards from a narrow gap between weathered reddish terracotta tiles, with one dark eye clearly visible.](/assets/images/2024/img_1369.jpg)

*A little lizard peering out from the terracotta. The garden residents have some very good hiding places.*
{: .media-caption }

And the lizards that hide in the garden getting watered - sorry little guy! Apart from all that, I started writing in a journal to capture some of my thoughts, and so far so good. It feels nice when awake at 3am to capture some of the things that are bouncing around in my head.

![Dark digital notebook page headed 'So what are my special interests?', listing cicadas and lizards, computers and modems, game consoles, science, and caring for nature.](/assets/images/2024/img_1371.jpg)

*Some notes from my new journal, trying to make sense of all those interests I've managed to collect over the years. Quite a list!*
{: .media-caption }

University has shown me even more great people and I just love being around them. I love listening. Not sure what it is. Now, I just need to wait for the 3rd October when I'll hopefully receive an offer for university in 2025. Fingers crossed! 🙃
